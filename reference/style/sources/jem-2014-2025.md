# JEM Style Report: Conventions of Journal of Educational Measurement Prose

Basis: 7 papers (jedm.12063, jedm.12124, jedm.12292, jedm.12334, jedm.12401, jedm.70001, jedm.70018). All quotations verbatim; en-dashes in originals render as "‐". Era split: 12063 (~2014) and 12124 (~2016) are mid-2010s; 12292, 12334 (~2021-22) transitional; 12401, 70001, 70018 (2024-25) newest.

## 1. Document Architecture

**Section sequence.** JEM tolerates two skeletons:

- *Empirical/simulation*: Abstract -> Introduction -> Method(s) -> Results -> Discussion -> Conclusion -> Limitations -> back matter. Method subsections are titled by content, verbatim examples: `## Method` / `### Models` / `### Data` / `#### NAEP fourth-grade mathematics and reading assessment restricted use data sets` / `### Design` / `### Evaluation Criteria` / `### Data Analysis` / `#### Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) Models` (12292). Both "Method" (12292) and "Methods" (12063) occur.
- *Methodological/theoretical*: named body sections replacing IMRaD: `## Formulation` / `### Notation and Assumptions` / `### Indices for Linked Scores` -> `## Predictive Utility Analysis` -> `## Examples of the Predictive Utility Analysis` -> `## Discussion and Conclusions` (70018); `## Analytical Results` (with numbered Scenario/Theorem/Proof blocks) -> `## Simulation Study With Changing Ability` -> `## Conclusion and Discussion` (12124).

**Introduction heading is era-marked.** 12063 opens the body under a topical heading (`## Multistage Testing`), 12124 and 70018 run the introduction unheaded directly after the abstract. All papers from ~2021 on use an explicit `## Introduction`.

**Two-part empirical architecture is now standard in modeling papers**: `## Empirical Illustration` (or `## Empirical Study`) then `## Simulation Study`, each with its own `### Analysis` / `### Results` (12401, 70001). Discussion then recapitulates both.

**Subsection habits**: Title Case; named for content, not function ("MST Panel Assembly", "Latent Dirichlet Allocation", "Topics from topic modeling"). Deep nesting to level #### in long papers (70001 has 13 level-#### headings under Results). Inside Results, bold run-in headings replace formal subheads: `**Item Parameter Estimates**. Tables 2 and 3 summarize the estimates...` (12401). A terminal `### Purpose` subsection may close the introduction (12292). Discussion carries its own subheads: `### Implications`, `### Limitations and Directions for Future Research` (12292).

**Back matter, newest era**: `## Acknowledgment(s)` (funding, named with grant numbers), `## Conflict of Interest` ("We have no known conflict of interest to disclose.", 12292), `## Declaration` (12401), `# Notes`/`# ENDNOTE`, `# Appendix`, `## References`.

**Abstract**: one unlabeled paragraph, 150-250 words, unstructured. Fixed move order: (1) context/problem, (2) gap or motivation, (3) what was done ("we introduced", "we developed", "we compare"), (4) design (empirical + simulation), (5) headline results, (6) implications sentence. Example closer: "Our findings suggest that the predictive utility analysis offers practical guidance for enhancing the use of linked scores as well as supporting institutional accountability." (70018).

## 2. Rhetorical Moves

**Openers** are definitional or practice-anchored, never narrative:
- "Multistage testing (MST) is a procedure designed to provide the benefits of adaptive testing -- enhanced measurement precision and efficiency -- without testing each examinee with a different set or different number of items." (12063)
- "Mixed-format tests include both multiple-choice (MC) items and constructed response (CR) items." (12292)
- "The field of educational measurement places validity and fairness as central concepts of assessment quality (AERA et al., 2014)." (12334)
- "For a certification, licensure, or placement exam, the pass rate is a key statistic that is closely monitored." (12124)
- "In U.S. colleges, admissions officers tend to use ACT-SAT concordant scores as predictions of individual scores for tests not taken (Pommerich, 2016)." (70018)

**Gap statements** (verbatim inventory):
- "However, the impact of the number of test retaking attempts on the pass rate itself, a crucially important statistic for many testing programs, has not been well studied." (12124)
- "However, there are few published studies in which researchers have systematically considered the impact of ignoring rater effects when they are present on estimates of student ability in large-scale mixed-format tests." (12292)
- "To our best knowledge, there exists no study that addresses the RS issue in the presence of cross-classified data or evaluates the consequences of ignoring/misspecifying the cross-classified structure." (12401)
- "Very little guidance appears in the literature about the effects of the choice of an estimator in practical applications." (12063)
- "The major problem in this situation is the use of linked scores without a thorough examination of their predictive utility (i.e., the degree to which they serve as predicted scores at the individual level)." (70018)

**Purpose statements**: "The purpose of this study is to determine which IRT proficiency estimators are the most effective for discovering examinees' true proficiency levels under various MST panel assembly conditions." (12063); "The purpose of the current study is to systematically explore the impacts of ignoring rater effects on student achievement estimates in mixed-format tests under conditions that reflect operational large-scale mixed-format assessments." (12292). Newest papers prefer direct agentive verbs: "we introduce a new modeling approach that accounts for RS when data have a cross-classified structure" (12401); "we developed a method, referred to as the 'predictive utility analysis,' for quantitatively evaluating the prediction accuracy and error properties of linked scores" (70018).

**Research questions**: formatted as a colon-led displayed list ("We focused on the following research question: What impact does _ignoring_ rater effects have on estimates of student achievement in mixed-format tests when rater effects are present?", 12292). Newest papers restate each RQ verbatim and italicized as the topic frame of a discussion paragraph: "For the first research question, _how effectively does the TTM account for local dependence for CR items in testlets_, this study found that..." (70001).

**Roadmap paragraph** closes the introduction: "The remainder of this article is organized as follows. In 'The Proposed Modeling Approach' section, we present specifications of the new modeling approach and introduce the estimation algorithm." (12401); 12334 does it in prose: "First, we discuss three views of fairness in relation to validity, followed by a justification... We then detail a structure for fairness argumentation... Then, we summarize the status of fairness investigations in AI assessments..."

**Contribution claims**: enumerated and explicit. "The contribution of this article is threefold. First, there has not been much discussion in the literature on different operational definitions of pass rate. This article fills the gap by clearly differentiating two definitions... Second, by analytical derivation, it provides the formulae... Third, the simulation study here offers insight on the magnitude of deviation from the baseline pass rate..." (12124). "Accordingly, our study makes a contribution to existing literature by using results from operational administrations of a large-scale mixed-format assessment to systematically explore the influence of ignoring rater effects..." (12292).

**Limitations framing**: a named subsection or a dedicated paragraph, opened by concession and closed by future-work directives: "Our study has some limitations that warrant additional consideration and further investigation in future studies." (12292); "Admittedly, there are other factors that play important roles in test retaking and pass rate -- for example, the lag time between first take and retakes." (12124); "We advise caution regarding generalization of results from this study, especially results from the simulation study, as testing programs differ substantially in their policy regarding test retakes..." (12124).

**Implications-for-practice language** is obligatory; the audience is named explicitly ("testing practitioners", "decision makers", "score users", "scoring leaders, raters"): "Our findings have several implications for researchers and practitioners who work with mixed-format assessments." (12292); "Institutions may strengthen their accountability by incorporating the predictive utility analysis into their decision-making processes." (70018); "In practice, we suggest that researchers and practitioners compare the results from analyses that control for rater severity and analyses that do not..." (12292).

## 3. Paragraph Patterns

Paragraphs open with a topic sentence carrying the paragraph's claim; evidence follows; no pyramid-of-suspense. Additive openers dominate: "In addition" (25), "Additionally," (17), "Furthermore," (11), "Moreover," (8) across the batch. Restatement is signaled by "In other words" (9): "In other words, when each student answered 30% or 50% of items, increasing test length led to a reduction in MAE values, but the impact was more pronounced when rater effects were considered..." (12292). Exemplification: "For example" / "For instance" mid-paragraph. Summative closers: "In sum", "In summary", "In conclusion", "Taken together, these results are fully consistent with the findings reported in our simulation study." (70018). Contrast turns open with "However," (35). Results paragraphs are chained to displays: nearly every results paragraph begins "Table N shows/presents/summarizes..." or "As shown in Figure N, ...".

## 4. Sentence-Level Grammar

**Person**: first-person plural "we" is universal in all eras, including procedures ("We simulated data from 2,000 examinees at each of 41 quadrature points", 12063; "we fitted three IRT models to the SET data", 12401). No first-person singular anywhere. Passive voice reserved for impersonal procedure and data genesis: "Eight MST forms were created to represent each of the eight assembly conditions" (12063); "The item response data for Tests X and Y were generated under the Rasch model" (70018). "This study/article/paper" serves as a sentence agent for findings: "this study found that the choice of Bayesian (prior) and non-Bayesian (no prior) estimators was of more practical significance..." (12063).

**Tense by section**: Introduction -- present for domain facts ("MST is a procedure"), present perfect for the literature ("researchers have proposed psychometric models that directly incorporate rater effects", 12292; "A few studies have investigated the effect of retest", 12124). Method -- simple past throughout. Results -- past for analytic outcomes ("Results from the mixed ANOVA indicated that...", 12292), present for display facts and durable truths ("Table 6 summarizes the biases and RMSEs", 12401; "Figure 1 presents scatter plots", 70001). Discussion -- present for claims ("suggests", "indicates", "provides"), past only when recalling what was done.

**Hedging inventory (verbatim, batch counts)**: may (120), tend(s) to (32), likely (24), could be (24), warrant(s) (21, incl. "warrant additional consideration", "warrants further study"), should be (16), relatively (11), generally (11), might (11), approximately (10), typically (9), appear(s) to (8), somewhat (5), caution (5). "suggest(s/ed) that" family ~44. Hedges cluster on generalization and interpretation, never on display facts. Typical: "Estimates of examinees' proficiency may be robust enough to shield them from the effects of misrouting, particularly in the two-stage MST design used in this study, unless MST modules include many ill-fitting items." (12063).

## 5. Lexicon

Characteristic nouns: testing program(s), operational assessment/administration, large-scale assessment, examinee(s), test taker(s), score users, decision makers, practitioners; measurement precision, estimation bias/error, model-data fit, parameter recovery, local (in)dependence, rater effects, cut score, pass rate, linking/equating/concordance, latent trait/construct, substantive scores, validity, fairness, reliability, accountability.

Characteristic verbs: estimate, calibrate, evaluate, examine, explore, investigate, manipulate, generate, fit (fitted), recover, address, account for, incorporate, yield, produce, exhibit, demonstrate, illustrate, inform.

Characteristic adjectives/adverbs: operational, psychometric, empirical, simulated, conditional, marginal, robust, salient ("a salient effect", 12292), substantial(ly), negligible/non-negligible ("would be nonnegligible, particularly for low- and high-performing examinees", 12063), systematic(ally).

Stock collocations with rough batch counts: "i.e." (84), "e.g." (77), "However," (35), "In addition" (25), "suggests that" + "suggest that" + "suggested that" (~44), "In this study" (18), "this article" (14), "this paper" (9), "Note that" (15), "In other words" (9), "we recommend" (7), "As shown in" (6), "The purpose of" (6), "consistent with" (5), "future studies" + "future research" (9), "upon request" (2, older papers only), "the current/present study" (6).

## 6. Math in Prose

Models are introduced for applied readers by a strict rhythm: (1) verbal motivation, (2) display equation, (3) numbered equation tag, (4) a "where" gloss that translates every symbol into domain language: "where @@eq0002@@ is the location of student _n_ on the construct (i.e., student ability), @@eq0003@@ is the difficulty of item _j_ on the logit scale (i.e., item difficulty)" (12292). Symbols always carry a parenthetical plain-language alias at first use. Derivations are interleaved with intuition sentences: "When the pass rate is defined as the percentage of test takers who pass the test, intuitively it can only go up when more attempts are allowed, because a larger-than-zero percentage of test takers will pass the test with one more attempt." (12124). Consequences are restated in words immediately after algebra: "In other words, examinees with ability higher than the cut will pass the test with probability 1, and examinees below the cut pass the test with 0 probability." (12124). Equations are cross-referenced by number ("By using Equations 4 and 8, the MSE of prediction for linked score L_X is calculated as", 70018). Heavy formalism is quarantined: theorems get numbered Scenario/Theorem/Proof blocks (12124); the rest of the paper stays verbal.

## 7. Reporting Results

Display-anchored narration: "As shown in Table 6, all parameters are well recovered in the four conditions considered in this simulation study." (12401); "Table 7 shows the Spearman's rank correlations between true and predicted substantive scores of students and instructors..." (12401); "Figure 4 presents the results from the four-way interaction..." (12292). Numeric ranges are reported inline in parentheses: "(1.04 <= MAE <= 1.12)"; "(.30 <= MAE <= .77)" (12292). Precision conventions: two decimals for bounded statistics; leading zero dropped in older and most newer papers (".05", ".95", "-.22") but retained in 70001 ("0.038", "0.0007") -- a visible APA-7-era drift. Evaluation criteria are justified by citation: "We used 0.06 as a cutoff for a salient effect (Cohen, 1988)." (12292). Non-interpretation is announced rather than silent: "Apart from the above-mentioned three-way interaction, the other factors that we manipulated as well as their interactions had effect sizes less than .06, which we used as a cutoff for a salient effect. Therefore, we did not interpret the results for these effects in detail." (12292). Space-saving is declared: "To create a compact table, the results are shown for only a subset of the simulation conditions." (70018); "therefore, we only present results of Items 1 and 2 to save space." (12401). Simulation outcomes are judged with stock verdicts: "well recovered", "good parameter and score recovery", "precisely estimated" ("The largest SE for student and instructor substantive slopes is .05", 12401). Decisions consequences are dramatized concretely: "Say, if an institution requires its instructors who scored below -1 ... to participant in a teaching training program, then based on the three models, 85, 102, and 76 instructors would need to take the additional training." (12401).

## 8. Citation Practices

Author-year, both narrative and parenthetical. Narrative for close engagement: "As Kolen and Tong () showed using real data sets from linear tests, this study found that..." (12063); "Patz et al. (1997) used a subset of data from the 1992 NAEP Trial State Assessment Program..." (12292). Dense parenthetical strings for territory-marking: "(e.g., Böckenholt, 2017; Kim & Bolt, 2021; Meiser et al., 2019; Park & Wu, 2019; Plieninger & Meiser, 2014)" (12401). "e.g."/"i.e." are constant (77/84); "cf." nearly absent (2); "see" reserved for URLs and cross-points ("(see www.ncsbn.org/NCLEX_Stats_2013.pdf for complete statistics)", 12124). Pin cites accompany quotations: "who state that when tests include '30 or more items, the inverse of the TCC provides a very accurate MLE of ability for the 3PL model.'" (12063, citing p. 137); "researchers: 'SHOULD [systematically] make the observed data used in simulations as complicated and "messy" as the real data...' (p. 75, emphases in the original)" (12292).

**Polite criticism** names the target and the defect in measured terms: "This is a post hoc approach, which is statistically suboptimal." (12292, of Shin et al. 2019); "Shin et al. (2019) drew a conclusion that rater effects do not exist based on a statistically suboptimal method." (12292); "The study by Patz et al. (1997) was based on quite limited simulation conditions, such that it did not fully reflect the characteristics of data collection designs in operational assessments, including NAEP." (12292); "Somewhat different from Kolen and Tong (), this study found that..." (12063).

**Open science**: no preregistration or data-availability statements in this batch. New-era markers instead: Conflict of Interest sections (12292), Declaration sections (12401), supplementary materials ("Online Supplement 1 includes these results", 12292; "see results in the supplementary material", 12401). Older papers share by request: "We will provide a full version of the article ... upon request." (12063); "Figures with m up to 100 are available upon request." (12124). Funding is granular: "This research was supported by Spencer Foundation #10018592." (12292); "through Grant R305A190079" (12334).

## 9. Tone and Taboos

Register: formal, plain, practitioner-facing. Contractions never occur. Hype is forbidden -- contributions are "useful", "helpful", "practical", never "groundbreaking". Direct advice in imperative or "we recommend/suggest/advise" is idiomatic: "We advise caution regarding generalization of results from this study" (12124); "We recommend considering RS and the cross-classification, if possible; otherwise, exercising caution in interpretation." (12401). The outer bound of color is a literary allusion with a citation: "One may say they are searching for a holy grail (Davies, 2010)." (12334). Rhetorical questions are rare but admitted in conceptual papers: "How can we revise the proposed approach of argument-based fairness to address this issue?" (12334). Out of place in JEM: first-person singular, contractions, exclamation, unhedged universal claims, journalism-style ledes, "novel/innovative" self-praise without a demonstrated comparison, undefined acronyms (every acronym is expanded at first use, "Many-Facet Rasch (MFR) model"), and statistical symbols without verbal glosses.

## 11. Stylistic Drift (mid-2010s -> 2024-25)

- Introduction heading: topical/unheaded (12063, 12124, 70018) -> explicit `## Introduction` standard (12292 on; 70018 retains the old unheaded habit, so drift is incomplete).
- Explicit `### Purpose` subsection and numbered/italicized research questions appear only post-2021; discussion structured by restating RQs verbatim is a 70001 habit.
- Back matter professionalizes: Conflict of Interest / Declaration sections, grant numbers, supplementary materials replace "available upon request" (12063, 12124) with "Online Supplement"/"supplementary material" (12292, 12401). Still no preregistration or data-availability statements.
- Number style: 70001 prints leading zeros ("0.038"); all earlier papers drop them (".05"). Watch this; APA 7 pushes toward retention but the field is mixed.
- Methods deepen: ####-level nesting, separate "Evaluation Criteria"/"Evaluation metrics" subsections, software named with versions and packages ("_rxtatix_ R package (Kassambara, 2020)", "flexMIRT(R)", "lme4").
- Architecture: the two-part "Empirical Illustration/Study + Simulation Study" pairing becomes the default for new-model papers (12401, 70001).
- Copyediting loosens in the newest papers: 70001 and 12401 contain visible agreement and article errors ("A Topic Testlet Model (TTM) integrates topic modeling within a psychometric framework was proposed", "would be treated as different students when he/she/they are in classes"). Do not imitate; the norm to hit is the cleaner 12292/12334 prose.
- Citation recency and interdisciplinarity grow: newest papers cite NLP/ML venues (Blei et al., LDA; fairness-in-AI literature in 12334) alongside the psychometric canon.
