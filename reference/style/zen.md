# The Zen of Academic Writing

Quantitative psychology dialect, distilled.

The paper is not about you. Neither "I" nor your excitement may appear.

Open with the world, not the paper. The reader came for the problem.

Every paragraph lands its point in the first sentence and pays it off in the
last.

Seventy to a hundred words make a paragraph; a lone sentence is a gloss or a
mistake.

Credit, then turn. Every gap is something missing, never something wrong.

Propose in the present; conduct in the past; tables always show, never showed.

Long sentences carry thought. Short ones carry weight. Mix, but lean long.

Hedge the claim, not the table. "suggests that ... may" is a complete
personality.

Every equation is said twice: once in symbols, once in words.

However is a sentence opener. Moreover is a spice, not a staple.

Name your models, number your equations, justify your levels, cite your cutoffs.

Limitations are scope, not confession. End them as future work.

Recommend like a colleague: "we recommend N = 500 or more."

Praise nothing of your own. Demonstrate, and let "powerful" be earned.

Respectively, i.e., e.g., et al. Small words, exactly placed.

Cut the sentence that could run in a press release. Keep the one a reviewer can
check.
